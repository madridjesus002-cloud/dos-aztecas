01 · People and place

Who were the Aztecs?

“Aztec” is a broad historical term associated with peoples of central Mexico. The Mexica of Mexico-Tenochtitlan formed a powerful alliance with Texcoco and Tlacopan, known as the Triple Alliance. Nahuatl was the dominant language among these allies.

Their world included farmers, artisans, traders, and complex cities. Maize, beans, squash, and chilies supported the food economy, while rain, agriculture, and the earth were deeply connected to religious life.

Aztec culture was part of a much wider Mesoamerican world. Its art and ideas drew on earlier societies, and its neighbors had their own languages, histories, and traditions. Mexican heritage cannot be reduced to one people or one period.

Source: The Aztec Empire · Guggenheim Bilbao / INAH ↗

Food

A foundation of Mexican cooking

Corn, beans, and chili form the foundation of the traditional Mexican food system. Tortillas are one expression of a larger culture of growing, preparing, and sharing food.

Community

Knowledge shared across generations

Traditional cuisine connects farming, culinary techniques, ritual practices, and community customs. Its value lives in the people who preserve and pass on this knowledge.

Continuity

A heritage that is still alive

UNESCO recognized traditional Mexican cuisine as intangible cultural heritage in 2010. Cooks and farming communities across Mexico continue to sustain and develop it today.

03 · From kernel to tortilla

What is nixtamalization?

A traditional process used across Mexico and Central America: dried maize is cooked and steeped in water with food-grade lime, then rinsed and ground into masa.

Lime means cal. Here, “lime” is food-grade calcium hydroxide, not the citrus fruit.

  1. 01

    Begin with dried maize

    The process starts with dried corn kernels.

  2. 02

    Cook with water and cal

    Heat and an alkaline solution begin changing the kernels’ structure.

  3. 03

    Let the kernels steep

    The corn rests in the cooking liquid. Methods and soaking times vary among communities.

  4. 04

    Rinse the nixtamal

    The treated kernels are drained and rinsed, removing loosened outer skins.

  5. 05

    Grind into masa

    The nixtamal is milled into dough for tortillas, tamales, and other foods.

Why the process matters

Nixtamalization changes corn’s flavor and processing properties. It can increase calcium content and make niacin, vitamin B3, more available to the body. It is both culinary knowledge and practical food science.

Source: CIMMYT · What is nixtamalization? ↗
